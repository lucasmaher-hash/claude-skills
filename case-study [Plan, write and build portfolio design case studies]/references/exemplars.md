# Exemplars

When asked for a good case-study reference, give **Calmsphere first**, then pick from the others
by project type.

## ★ Karen-Happuch: CalmSphere (go-to example)

https://karenhappuch.com/calmsphere · Industrial design + companion app · A' Design Award
runner-up 2026 · Framer, Apple-white (`#1D1D1F` on `#FFFFFF`, `#E8E8ED` surfaces, `#0088FF`
accent), SF Pro / Inter.

Order: hero ("Calm Sphere" set huge with the rendered product cutting the words) → award badge →
**Project Overview** (What is it · User problem · My role · What I designed · Outcome or
validation) → product in hand + app → Problem statement with two hard numbers → Desk research
cards **each with a citation** → Empathy map → Design solution (labelled part callouts) →
Competitor analysis (strengths/weaknesses) → 2×2 effectiveness × accessibility with the product
alone top-right → Insights → sketches → CAD/renders → lifestyle block with colour variants →
paper wireframes → UI components → UI iterations → final UI → Next steps → Tools.

**Take:** the overview block; hardware and app told as one product; cited desk research; a 2×2
where the product owns a quadrant; product-page staging (hero, colours, lifestyle) on top of
process.

**Don't take (checked 2026-10-23):** "Insights" shows 47 % / 62 % / 36 % / 54 % with no source,
reading like product outcomes while the next steps still list a pilot test. Two competitor cards
share identical copy. "Refined through user feedback" never says how many or what changed.
The empathy map leads to no decision. There is no pivot or dead end, and the UI iterations are
shown without reasons.

## Jason Yuan: "I Got Rejected by Apple Music… So I Redesigned It" (2017)

Unsolicited redesign, ~3,250 words, 32 figures (7 GIFs). Opens with a story (the rejection) and
defuses it at once. Places the product's history before critiquing it. Announces three steps and
follows them. Research → one spectrum (Hoarders vs Nomads) that dictates strategy. Every change
has a source (an interview quote); every non-change has a reason. One-sentence principle.
Captions with personality. Motion only on interactions. Honest ending about his own skill.

## Jason Yuan: "Introducing Mercury OS" (2019)

Speculative OS, never built. Guiding metaphor (Mercury: liquid, messenger, closest to the sun).
Shows failed attempts and names the breakthrough moment ("I was asking the wrong questions").
Personal stake + a hard number ("People like me." / context switches cost up to 15 minutes).
Three alliterative principles, each with a testable consequence. Vocabulary small→large
(Module → Flow → Space), each term defined once with an example, four diagrams. Named art
direction ("Kiri"). Open ending, 30+ people credited.

## Jessica Im: TurboTax pre-filled onboarding (imjiwoo.com/tt-onboarding, 2025)

Shipped, internship, ~950 words. Header with timeline, role, launch date and every teammate's
function. Fact bar first ("first month after launch": 3 iterations, +7.5 % filing rate, 9,406
uploads). Problem as a number (17 % drop-off; 20 questions vs 3). Options compared, choice
justified; survey of 158 non-customers. Constraints admitted ("not perfect, but the best of the
compatible"). Post-launch failure told (11 % errors → −10.5 %). Section nav at top. Generous,
measured spacing (see visuals.md).

## Deji Ajetomobi: YesCheff (dejiajetomobi.com/design/yescheff)

Media carries, text labels. Organised by the user's path (create, cook, hands-free, save & pay,
promo). Fixed block per feature: short paragraph, demo video, three labelled details. One
product principle as a trust signal (only uses what's in the video transcript; refuses to invent
ingredients). Project info at the end. Launch videos as proof.

## Deji Ajetomobi: Finy (dejiajetomobi.com/design/finy-media-1)

Brand case study in ~150 words: one idea ("Path to Freedom"), every visual element traced back
to it, motion credited to a named collaborator.

## What all good ones share

One main point sayable in a sentence · visible structure · every image has a job (motion for
behaviour, stills for structure) · honesty where it hurts (rejection, dead end, post-launch
error, missing skill) · clear role even in a team.

## Lessons from real rebuilds (lucasmaher.com, 2026)

- A **positioning quadrant** was rejected on sight as generic-deck; replaced by three plainly
  written constraints with evidence reused from the problem section.
- **Pilot results placed under "The idea"** read backwards (results before the build) and
  duplicated the results section: moved out.
- The **finished UI shown before the pivot** flattened the pivot's reveal; the idea section was
  given wireframes instead, and the rejected v1 appears once, as the comparison.
- A **colour/type/character specimen section** competed with the final-design section; folded
  into one paragraph explaining *why* the look changed.
- **Problem evidence as a recreated real artefact** (the group chat that started the project)
  plus one stat bar beat a four-bar chart and a funnel.
- **Quotes as prose with key phrases in bold** read better than quote cards.
- **The cohort stated once** at the top of results; every chart below belongs to it.
- **Cramped spacing** was the most visible quality problem; measured spacing from a reference
  fixed it.
