# Evidence

A study earns space only if it **caused a decision** or **proves a claim on the page**.

## Study template

Use for every piece of evidence in a plan:

```
### Study N: <name>
- Why:        which claim on the page it proves
- How:        method, n, duration, exact questions/tasks
- Measure:    the numbers or observations collected
- Show:       the visual it becomes (fact bar, funnel, chart, table, quote)
- Example:    the SHAPE of a result, clearly labelled as an example value
- Page change: what moves, gets cut or gets reordered because of it
```

Then draw a map: study → section. Any section with no arrow into it is an unproven claim.

## Methods that fit student and solo projects

| Method | Proves | Cost | Show as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-observation, filmed** (e.g. cook 3 recipes, log every phone check) | The problem, as a measured fact, n=1 named plainly | An afternoon | Interruption timeline + one stat |
| **Short survey** (~100, 5 questions) | It isn't only you; what matters most | A day | Fact row + one sorted bar chart |
| **Diary study** (~12 people, 5 days) | What the problem costs (time, messages, failures) | A week | Funnel: attempts → agreed → done |
| **Preference test v1 vs final** (~30, randomised, semantic differential 1–7) | Why the pivot | A day | Side-by-side + two crossing lines |
| **Variant test** (3 options, ~20 people, time on task + errors) | The hardest design choice | Two days | Variants in a row, metrics under, winner in accent |
| **Video concept test** (no hardware needed) | Reaction to an unbuildable concept, honestly labelled | A day | One big number + 2–3 concern quotes |
| **Pilot with a coded prototype** (friend groups, 2–3 weeks, usage logs) | Which features matter; outcome | Weeks | Share-of-time chart → reorders the page |
| **Moderated usability test** (~12, 5 tasks, SUS) | Iteration worked | Two days | Task table before/after + one SUS number |
| **Accessibility audit** | Craft and care | Hours | Measured values, fixes made, trade-offs stated |
| **Store data / reviews** (shipped apps) | Real-world result | Free | Numbers with timeframe, quoted reviews |
| **Explain a mechanism** (no test) | A system decision the reader can respect | Hours | One step diagram |

## Projects without metrics

Three things replace numbers: a sharply drawn problem, a stated reason for every decision, and
honest learnings. Answer clearly *which problem, who benefits, what actually changed*. Two of the
strongest case studies (Yuan's) shipped nothing and contain no usage number.

## Honesty rules

- Example numbers in plans are marked as examples and **replaced or removed** before shipping.
- Every number: source + timeframe + n. Desk-research stats get citations (Calmsphere does this
  well for its research cards).
- **Never let general literature numbers pose as your product's outcome.** "47 % anxiety
  reduction" under "Insights" on an untested prototype is the classic mistake.
- Status phrased exactly: "submitted" vs "on the App Store"; "desktop simulation, not tested in
  a headset".
- A recreated artefact (e.g. a group chat rewritten from real chats) is fine as illustration;
  don't present invented content as a real quote.
- Team findings: credit who ran them.
