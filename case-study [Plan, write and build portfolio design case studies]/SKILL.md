# Case study

A case study is not a gallery of final screens. Reviewers are checking whether the designer can
**spot a problem, make defensible decisions and move a product forward**. Process and reasoning
count for more than polish. Everything in this skill serves that one test.

Hard facts to design around:
- A portfolio gets **30–60 s** on first pass; a single case study gets **5–10 min**. Everything
  essential must survive the 30-second version (headings + captions + images alone).
- **~800 words where every sentence counts beat 4,000.** Target 900–1,100 words of prose.
- A portfolio is judged by its **weakest** case study. Three to five strong ones, not eight.

## Workflow

1. **Interview first, never guess.** Ask (one at a time, recommend an answer each time) what you
   cannot read from the project files: context and dates, the user's exact role and each
   teammate's part, status (shipped / prototype / concept), the problem in their words, the
   biggest decision or pivot and why, what evidence exists (numbers, feedback, recordings,
   sketches, v1 screens), what must be left out, and voice. Record answers in a table.
2. **Find the spine.** One sentence the whole page serves: *"X lets [user] [do thing] — so
   [pain] stops."* Separate the **product insight** (spine) from the **best story** (usually the
   pivot, which becomes the centrepiece section, not the spine).
3. **Choose the structure.** See `references/structure.md`. Default skeleton: hero + one-liner +
   overview block → problem → the one idea → the key decision/pivot → system & craft (2–3 hard
   details) → result & learnings. Give each section **exactly one job**.
4. **Plan the evidence.** For every section: what proves it? Use `references/evidence.md`. Each
   study gets why / how / measure / show / example shape / **what it changes on the page**. A
   study that changes nothing on the page does not get space.
5. **Plan the visuals.** `references/visuals.md`: 30-second path, before/after, motion only for
   behaviour, one message per chart, captions that state findings.
6. **Write.** `references/writing.md`: finding → decision, first person for decisions, say what
   did NOT change and why, at most three learnings.
7. **Critique before shipping** with the checklist below. When reviewing someone's page, report
   the verdict first, then the 3–5 changes that matter most, then the rest.

For a full plan document, copy `assets/plan-template.md` and fill it in.

## Non-negotiables

- **No invented research, numbers, personas or quotes.** Example values in a plan are labelled
  as examples and replaced before shipping. n=1 stated plainly beats a faked study, and
  reviewers spot the difference. Unsourced statistics presented like outcomes are the most
  common flaw even in good portfolios (see the Calmsphere notes in `references/exemplars.md`).
- **Don't open with the aesthetic.** Problem first, unless one sentence of problem follows the
  hook immediately.
- **Don't bury the key decision** in a "challenges" section at the bottom. It gets the most
  space and the biggest image.
- **Name roles precisely** in team work. Never claim the whole thing.
- **Don't explain the tech.** The reader is hiring a designer: one caption covers the tools.
- **Name honest weaknesses** (n=1, no metrics, AI-assisted build, never tested in context).
  Said first, they protect the rest of the page.

## Checklist

- [ ] The main point fits in one sentence, in the hero
- [ ] Overview block answers: what is it · user problem · my role · what I designed · outcome/validation
- [ ] Role exact; teammates' parts named
- [ ] Fact bar of 2–4 checkable numbers or facts (no metrics → verifiable facts: "On the App Store", "3 iterations", "Solo, 6 weeks")
- [ ] Problem is the strongest paragraph, source named honestly
- [ ] Research condensed into one model that forces a decision (not a persona, not a decorative 2×2)
- [ ] Key decision shown before/after, same crop and size
- [ ] At least one dead end or setback shown
- [ ] Every animation shows behaviour, not decoration
- [ ] Every caption carries a finding
- [ ] 30-second test passes (images + captions + headings tell the story)
- [ ] Every number has a source and a timeframe; no outcome claimed that wasn't measured
- [ ] At most three learnings, honestly phrased; open questions allowed
- [ ] Under ~1,100 words of prose; no two paragraphs without an image
